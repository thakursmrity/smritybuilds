Bringing back the weekday lunch

A strategy to lift order frequency on Zomato, worked from diagnosis to rollout.

01 · The brief

Growth is slowing. Where exactly?

The prompt: leadership says order growth is slowing, build a strategy to fix it. Before solving anything, split the headline number into the levers that make it up.

Total orders = Active users × Orders per active user

Questions asked first

  • Is the slowdown from fewer active users, or fewer orders per user?
  • Is frequency dropping within segments, or only because the user mix changed?
  • Which markets: metros, tier 2, or both?

What the data says

  • Active users are still growing
  • Orders per user is falling, within segments
  • The drop is concentrated in metros

The broken lever

Frequency

Acquisition works. Existing users are ordering less often.

01 · Diagnosis

A falling average can be good news or bad news

Overall frequency is a weighted average of segments, so the same drop can come from opposite causes. Starting point: 1,000 users placing 4,000 orders a month.

SegmentUsersOrders per userOrders
Heavy200102,000
Light8002.52,000
Total1,0004.04,000
Scenario A · Mix shift

500 new light users join and nobody changes behavior. Frequency falls from 4.0 to 3.5, but total orders rise from 4,000 to 5,250. That is healthy growth, and the job becomes turning new users into regulars.

Scenario B · Real decline

Same users, but light users drop from 2.5 to 1.5 orders a month. Frequency falls to 3.2 and total orders fall to 3,200. Existing users are ordering less. This is the case being solved here.

Always check segments before concluding. Only segment level data tells you which story is true.

02 · Goal

One goal, with a number and guardrails

Raise orders per monthly transacting user from 3.2 to 3.8 in six months in metro cities, without lowering order value or adding notification fatigue.

Definition

Order frequency = completed orders in a month ÷ users who placed at least one order that month. Cancelled and refunded orders are excluded, and the monthly window matches the natural food delivery cycle.

Why not other goals

  • More users: already growing
  • Higher order value: does not fix ordering less often
  • Same restaurant repeats: too narrow

Guardrails

  • Average order value falls no more than 5%
  • Notification opt out rate stays flat
  • Cancellation rate stays flat
03 · Segments

Diagnose with behavior, solve with occasion

A strong segment combines two or three lenses: where the drop is, who the users are, and why they order.

Chosen: lapsing working professionals in metros who used to order weekday lunch

  • Built in habit: lunch happens five days a week, so the ceiling is high
  • Proven intent: they ordered before, so winning back is cheaper than creating demand
  • Something changed: and that change is findable
  • Direct goal fit: every recovered lunch is one more order per user

Fully within product control and fast to test. It slows the order more than it stops it.

Daily cost

Stops the order outright and explains the lapse. Only partly in Zomato's hands, since restaurants set menu prices.

06 · Problem statements

Two problems worth solving

Problem 1 · Decision fatigue

For lapsing working professionals in metros who used to order weekday lunch, deciding what to eat every day is tiring because the app shows many options that feel repetitive, so they default to the canteen, which needs no decision. This lowers orders per user.

Insight: the canteen wins on zero effort to decide, not on taste or price.

Problem 2 · Daily cost

For the same professionals, the rising daily cost of lunch, with fewer discounts and added fees, makes Zomato feel unaffordable as a daily habit, so they switch to canteens and nearby shops on most days. This lowers orders per user.

Constraint: no fixing it with discounts, which would break the order value guardrail.

Weekdays, 11:30 to 1:30

A card on home shows your usual, its price and delivery time.

Tap to order

Done in one tap.

Not today?

Five suggestion cards, like a friend offering options. Swipe right to order, left for next.

Skip

Hides for the day. Turn it off any time in settings.

In the MVP

  • Card on home only, no push
  • Suggestions from past orders and top rated lunch spots nearby
  • Only restaurants delivering in under 30 minutes

Left for later

  • Weekly planning
  • Learning from swipes
  • Push reminders

Who sees it first

10% of lapsing professionals in one metro, against a control group. It tests the core bet: does removing the decision bring them back?

Lunch Pass: lower the daily cost, build the habit

Prepaying lowers the price per meal and pulls users back daily, so it moves cost and frequency together.

The offer

  • 10 weekday lunches
  • About 20% cheaper per meal than ordering normally
  • Valid for 30 days

Why restaurants agree

  • Guaranteed, predictable volume
  • Fills spare kitchen capacity at midday
  • Fixed menu means simpler prep

In the MVP

  • 15 to 20 partner restaurants in office zones of one metro
  • Fixed lunch menu, weekdays only
  • Offered to lapsing professionals only

Two risks that sound alike but are not: concentration is about who gets the orders (restaurants can lose even while totals grow). Cannibalization is about whether orders are new at all (totals stay flat while the feature looks busy). The first is tracked by the share of orders going to the top 10% of restaurants, the second only by a control group.

Quick Lunch tradeoffs

Speed versus discovery. Personal versus fair. Visible versus annoying.

Lunch Pass tradeoffs

Price versus margin. Commitment versus flexibility. Volume versus restaurant health.

10 · Rollout

Fast experiment first, bigger bet second

Phase 1 · Weeks 1 to 4

Test Quick Lunch

10% of lapsing professionals in one metro, with a control group.

Continue if lunch orders per user rise versus control and turn off rate stays under 5%. Stop if there is no lift after four weeks, or turn off rate passes 10%.

Phase 2 · Weeks 5 to 12

Pilot Lunch Pass

Pass in two office zones with 15 to 20 restaurants. Quick Lunch expands to half the segment and starts learning from swipes.

Continue if redemption is above 70%, repurchase above 30%, and margin is positive.

Phase 3 · Months 4 to 6

Combine and expand

Pass lunches show up inside Quick Lunch. Expand to top metros, then add office batch delivery and employer wallets.

Target: orders per user from 3.2 to 3.8.

Takeaways

What this case taught

Diagnose before solving

A falling average can mean growth or decline. Split the metric and check segments first.

Pick, do not list

Scoring makes the tradeoff visible. Impact and solvability are scored separately.

Find what the alternative wins on

The canteen beats the app on effort to decide and on cost, not on food.

Measure against control

High adoption can hide zero new orders. Only a control group shows real lift.

Run the cheap experiment first, learn what brings people back, then place the bigger bet.
